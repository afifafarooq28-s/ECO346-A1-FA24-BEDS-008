Question 2: Education Spending Distribution

Key results

Number of students: 9,173
Unweighted median spending: Rs 7,000
Weighted median spending: Rs 6,600
Weighted 10th percentile: Rs 2,000
Weighted 90th percentile: Rs 30,000
90th-to-10th percentile ratio: 15.00
Observations excluded from the plotted range: 3


Interpretation

Most students spend relatively small amounts on education
annually, while a smaller number spend much more.
The weighted median is Rs 6,600, slightly lower than the 
unweighted median of Rs 7,000. This difference occurs because 
the household weights adjust the sample to better represent the 
rural Punjab population.
The 90th percentile is Rs 30,000, which is 15 times the 10th
percentile of Rs 2,000, showing substantial variation in 
education spending.


Question 3: Literacy by Age and Sex

Key results

Age 15–19: Men's literacy rate is 79.8%, compared with 73.1%
for women.
Age 55–59: Men's literacy rate is 50.8%, compared with 15.0%
for women.
Largest gender gap: 35.8 percentage points in the 55–59 age
group.


Interpretation

Literacy rates are higher among men than women across all age
groups in rural Punjab. The difference is relatively small 
among younger people but becomes much larger in older age groups.
For ages 15–19, men's literacy is 79.8%, compared with 73.1% for 
women.Among those aged 55–59, men's literacy is 50.8%, while
women's literacy is only 15.0%, creating the largest gap of 
35.8 percentage points.However, this figure cannot explain why
these gender differences exist or establish whether age itself
causes lower literacy.
